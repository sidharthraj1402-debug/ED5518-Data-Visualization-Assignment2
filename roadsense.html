How it's measured +

Road

  1. Up–down jolt: the accelerometer minus a 1 s gravity average, projected on gravity, so the phone can lie any way up.
  2. GPS distance cut into 100 m stretches. Each gets the RMS of the jolt, and its average speed v.
  3. R = RMS × √(30 km/h ÷ v) × vehicle factor (car 1.0, bus 0.8, 2-wheeler 0.7, auto 0.6). Vibration grows about with √speed on the same road (ISO 8608 road spectra).
  4. Ride Quality Score 0–100 from R (0 → 100, 0.6 → 85, 1.2 → 60, 2.0 → 35, 3.5 → 0), minus 10 per pothole. Good ≥ 85, Fair ≥ 60, Poor ≥ 35, else Very poor. Under 10 km/h is not rated.
  5. A pothole is a jolt over the vehicle's threshold (car 3.5 … auto 5 m/s²), pinned at the GPS position.

Comfort

  1. A 3 s gravity average gives up–down. Forward is the phone's long edge, or its back in a holder; sideways is at right angles. Gravity almost freezes during a push, so braking isn't mistaken for a tilt.
  2. Movement slower than 0.3 s is a push (braking, starting, turning). The rest is vibration.
  3. ISO 2631-1 total value av = √(1.4²ax² + 1.4²ay² + az²) over 5 s, against its six comfort bands. No frequency weighting, so it's an estimate.

Driving

  1. A harsh move is a push over 2.5 m/s² for ≥ 0.3 s. Mostly sideways means a sharp turn. Forward–back is a brake or a start, told apart by GPS speed before and after, or by the forward direction learned from GPS.
  2. Speeding: seconds more than 3 km/h over the limit you set.
  3. Score = 100 − 3 per hard brake − 2 per hard start − 2 per sharp turn − 1 per 10 s speeding.

Limits, honestly

  • The road score is this app's own index in the spirit of the Pavement Condition Index (PCI), not a calibrated IRI. Compare trips made with the same vehicle and phone.
  • About 60 motion readings a second; GPS is good to about 5–10 m.
  • Picking up the phone looks like a pothole or a harsh move. Leave it resting, and mark speed breakers in the report.
